# Driftline map themes

The reference for what belongs at each water: its story, place and culture, palette, materials, creatures, music and home. Anything new at a water (a home, a home part, a creature, a moment, a sound, a decoration) is checked against its entry here first.

Written 2026-11-22 at the director's request ("more details, so you have better references for when we add the new homes"; "a color palette description too, to each map"; "culture references or inspiration for what's included, even the music"). The facts come from the game itself: the Map card text and depth, the scene's own colours at day, dusk and night (`V3PAL`), the platform, what moves by day and at night (the map standard, checked by `__mapQC`), and each water's fish. The **home direction** and **home colours** are starting points for batch 5, not decisions: every home idea is approved by the director before it is drawn.

## Rules for every water
- **2D side view only:** everything is drawn as seen from the side. A roof is a roof profile, a boat is a hull outline, a deck is a plank edge with posts. Nothing is a front or top view squeezed in.
- **Glow Diorama:** rich glow lighting, crisp layered depth (far, middle, near), rim light on edges, bloom on lights, everything gently alive. Nothing floats or looks pasted on: whatever stands somewhere touches what it stands on (Starfall Lake and Circuit Lake are the two places where floating is the theme).
- **Cozy and homey, light humour.** The joke waters (Animal Farm, The Buffet, Corporate Pond, Circuit Lake) keep their gag, but the gag sits on a cozy, believable place.
- **Fishing stays the focus:** nothing covers the fishing area, and nothing busy moves right where the line and bobber are.
- **Homes (from the director's Willow Pond notes):** every home has the same format: 6 parts (light, deck, supports, rail, canopy and a seat), at least 16 colours shared by every part and taken from the water's own palette, canopies drawn in true side view (the patio umbrella is the model), everything placed on the deck (never on the rails), supports running down into the water and fading there, with a subtle flow past them (no growing and shrinking rings).
- **One water, one world:** materials, colours, creatures and sounds come from that water's place and culture. A palm tree doesn't go on Frostbite Lake; a neon sign doesn't go at Willow Pond.
- **Inspiration, never copies:** real places, crafts, art styles and music traditions are the inspiration. Nothing copies a specific film, game, song or artwork.

## How each entry is laid out
- **On the card:** the player's description and depth, word for word.
- **Place and story:** where it is and what it feels like.
- **Culture and inspiration:** the real-world places, crafts, art and traditions it draws on.
- **Palette:** a written description; the scene's real colours (sky top → horizon, water top → deep, land, lights) at day, dusk and night; and a 16-colour starting set for its home.
- **Materials:** what things here are made of.
- **Life:** what moves by day and at night today; its fish (common · rare · legendary · mythic examples).
- **Belongs / doesn't belong.**
- **Music and ambience:** what the music takes its cues from (instruments, style, tempo) and the background sounds.
- **Home today and home direction:** today's platform and starting ideas for its format home.

- **Homes (batch 5):** each home's six parts (light, deck, supports, rail, canopy, seat) are chosen from this water's materials. Its colour list starts from the 16 home colours above (more can be added from the same palette). The idea goes to the director for approval before it is drawn.
